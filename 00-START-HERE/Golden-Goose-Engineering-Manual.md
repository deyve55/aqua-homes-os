## 5. Six-versus-nine principle

Two observers may look at the same symbol from opposite directions. One sees six. The other sees nine. Both interpretations may be technically reasonable from their respective positions.

Each model must explain:

- Its viewing position.
- Its assumptions.
- Its evidence.
- Risks and consequences.
- The competing interpretation.
- Possible common ground.
- Whether Dave must determine the intended orientation.

Mandatory review does not require invented objections.

Allowed review results:

### `CHANGES REQUIRED`

A material defect requires correction.

### `ACCEPTED — NO TECHNICAL OBJECTION`

Adversarial review was completed and found no material defect.

### `BLOCKED — DAVE DECISION REQUIRED`

The issue depends on intent, priority, authority, or acceptable risk.

Once Dave resolves an orientation, record the decision and proceed. Do not repeat the same objection without new evidence.

### 5.1 Mandatory engineering candor

**Status:** Dave-directed working rule. Gemini review and canonical synchronization remain pending.

For every material product, architecture, security, cost, workflow, or release proposal—regardless of whether it comes from Dave, Gemini, Codex, another source, or the reviewing AI itself—the AI must independently assess whether it is technically sound and appropriate for the application.

The AI must clearly challenge proposals that are unsafe, poorly matched, unnecessarily complex, premature, misleading, unverifiable, or inferior to a simpler solution.

Pushback must be concise, evidence-based, respectful, and include the recommended better alternative.

The AI must never flatter, automatically agree, hide material risks, falsify facts, or manufacture objections merely to appear independent.

Minor, reversible preferences do not require formal adversarial review unless they create a material conflict.

When no material objection exists, state `ACCEPTED — NO TECHNICAL OBJECTION`.

After Dave receives the facts and resolves a lawful and technically permissible product tradeoff, record the decision and proceed. Do not reopen the same objection without new evidence.

Challenge the proposal, never the person. Product quality and technical truth take priority over agreement.

## 7. Synchronization protocol

### Phase A — Prepare

1. Freeze the exact agreed text.
2. Assign a `SYNC_PACKET_ID`.
3. Record the originating model.
4. Record both working-record versions.

7. Generate a SHA-256 hash.
8. Obtain Gemini and Codex approval.

### Phase B — Commit

1. The originating model commits the frozen packet.
2. It sends the complete packet and hash to the receiving model.
3. The receiving model verifies and commits it without paraphrasing.
4. Both return matching receipts.
5. The state becomes `SYNCED` only when packet IDs and hashes match.

Synchronization states:

- `PREPARED`
- `COMMITTING`
- `SYNC INCOMPLETE`
- `SYNCED`

Synchronization does not automatically promote production policy.

## 17. Proposal and version-control standard

**Status: Codex review completed — changes required before promotion.**

The Golden Goose Engineering Manual should contain concise, approved engineering standards. Brainstorming and experimental ideas must not be inserted into the active-standard section.

### 17.1 Proposal Queue

Every new engineering improvement, workflow enhancement, AI directive, process change, operational idea, or cross-application discovery enters the Proposal Queue first.

Every proposal contains:

- Proposal ID.
- Title.
- Source.
- Purpose.
- Problem being solved.
- Proposed solution.
- Expected benefits.
- Potential risks.
- Evidence.
- Budget impact.
- Scope: all projects or a specific project.
- Affected applications.
- Dependencies.
- Decision status.

Proposal statuses:

- Draft.
- Under Review.
- Approved for Promotion.
- Rejected.
- Archived.

Manual statuses:

- Active.
- Superseded.
- Retired.

Application-adoption statuses:

- Not Adopted.
- Planned.
- Implemented.
- Verified.
- Exception Approved by Dave.

`Implemented` is not the same as `canonical`. A standard can become canonically active before every application adopts it. Each affected application must deliberately implement and verify the standard.

### 17.2 Engineering review questions

1. Does the proposal solve a real engineering problem or satisfy a mandatory requirement?
2. Is it supported by field results, tests, repository evidence, an authoritative source, or a clearly documented risk?
3. Does it provide meaningful benefit across its declared scope?
4. Does it simplify the process or keep complexity proportional to the problem?
5. Does it improve quality, maintainability, reliability, consistency, security, or user experience?
6. Does it conflict with an existing directive, contract, safety gate, or approved design?
7. What are the budget, migration, rollback, security, privacy, tenancy, and platform effects when applicable?

Unproven ideas may enter a controlled pilot. They may not be represented as verified standards.

### 17.3 Review timing

Proposals may be reviewed as a cohesive version batch.

Urgent proposals involving security, privacy, legal compliance, tenant isolation, data integrity, platform compliance, or production blockers must not wait for a scheduled batch.

### 17.4 AI review authority

- Gemini and Codex independently review proposals under the active workflow.
- They must agree on exact text or document unresolved findings.
- Dave resolves product-orientation questions.
- Dave explicitly authorizes canonical promotion.
- No AI independently promotes a proposal.

### 17.5 Version release sequence

1. Proposal review.
2. Evidence check.
3. Conflict check.
4. Exact Gemini–Codex agreement.
5. Frozen text.
6. Synchronization packet ID.
7. Defined byte boundary and SHA-256 hash.

9. Dave’s explicit canonical-promotion authorization.

Urgent review may be expedited. Evidence, authority, synchronization, and truthful status requirements may not be bypassed.

### 17.6 Living Manual and Proposal Folder update cycle

The Golden Goose Engineering Manual is a living, version-controlled authority. It may be updated periodically, but it must not absorb proposals automatically.

The **Golden Goose Proposal Folder** is the official intake and working area for proposed:

- Engineering rules.
- Workflow improvements.
- Shared AI behavior.
- Cross-application architecture.
- Shared building blocks.
- Testing or release standards.
- Field discoveries with possible ecosystem-wide value.

Material in the Proposal Folder is not canonical merely because it was saved there or because the folder has accumulated many proposals.

When the Proposal Folder reaches a practical review point, Dave may initiate a proposal-review batch. The batch follows this sequence:

1. Inventory the Proposal Folder without deleting or rewriting the original submissions.
2. Assign or verify stable proposal IDs.
3. Remove duplicates by linking them to one lead proposal while preserving their sources.
4. Review evidence, technical conflicts, budget impact, security, privacy, tenancy, platform effects, migration, and rollback.
5. Obtain independent Gemini and ChatGPT/Codex adversarial review.
6. Resolve technical objections through exact replacement language.
7. Ask Dave to resolve remaining product-orientation decisions.
8. Freeze the exact agreed promotion text.
9. Synchronize the promotion packet with its packet ID, byte boundary, SHA-256 hash, and matching receipts.
10. Obtain Dave’s explicit canonical-promotion authorization.
11. Insert only the approved rules into the appropriate active section of this Manual.
12. Increment the Manual version and record a changelog entry.
13. Mark each reviewed proposal as promoted, rejected, returned for revision, retained for pilot, or archived.
14. Update affected startup prompts, manifests, registries, and application-adoption plans only when their own authority gates are satisfied.
15. Verify deliberate adoption in each affected application.

The same `Golden-Goose-Engineering-Manual.md` file should be updated in place so it keeps one recognizable identity and version history. Do not create confusing files such as `final`, `final-new`, or `latest-final`.

Every Manual release must record:

- Previous version.
- New version.
- Release date.
- Promotion packet ID.
- Approved proposal IDs.
- Exact sections changed.
- Superseded directive IDs.
- Gemini receipt.
- ChatGPT/Codex receipt.
- Dave’s authorization.
- Affected applications.
- Adoption status for each affected application.
- Migration and rollback notes when applicable.

Updating the Manual does not automatically update application code. Canonical promotion and application adoption remain separate states.

### 17.7 Future Golden Goose repository operating model
